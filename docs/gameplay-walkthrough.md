# Gameplay Walkthrough: From Associate to Head of Family

Status: concept-level description written 2026-09-21 from the decisions in `docs/brainstorm-requirements.md`. It shows what a run feels like at each rank. Numbers are targets for design, not tuned values. Adding the rank durations gives 210 to 330 turns before the seat and roughly 300 to 360 monthly turns for a long tenure as head, so a full career is about 500 to 700 turns plus crisis weeks; the brief's earlier estimate of 400 to 600 is revised here. Interface labels follow decision 17: Italian term with English in parentheses.

---

## A new game

The setup screen asks for four things: a seed (or a random one), a starting place (a Palermo neighborhood or a provincial town, or let the seed decide), a character background (born into a family, or an outsider who proved useful), and a difficulty preset that scales the state's aggressiveness, Attention decay and flip rates. Ironman is a toggle.

The generator builds Sicily on its real outline: fixed major cities, generated towns and neighborhoods from archetypes, families with territories and districts, a provincial Commission in the player's province, simplified families elsewhere. It then runs fifteen to twenty years of pre-history. When the player arrives, the newspaper archive already holds a war, a few funerals, a contract scandal and the names of the men who matter. The player's own province is the only one simulated in detail; the rest of the island exists as families that trade, ally and feed events.

The first screen is a pixel-art street: the player's block, the sponsor's bar, a handful of shops, a card game in a back room. A short scene introduces the sponsor, a man of honor who has vouched for you. He does not explain the rules. He tells you what he expects on Sunday.

## Where you start decides how you earn

The starting place is not scenery. Each archetype has its own small payers for the associate weeks, its own crew-chief rackets, and one institution that is the prize. The tutorial beats are drawn from that economy: the late payer is a stallholder in the market quarter, a boat owner on the coast, a site foreman on the hillside.

| Start | First weeks as an associate | Crew chief money | The institution to take |
|---|---|---|---|
| Palermo harbor quarter | Tax on porters and dockers' bars, a night-shift card game, pilfering from cargo | Dock labor cooperative, fish market stalls, warehouse rents, a landing for contraband | The port: labor, customs, fish market, warehouses |
| Palermo market quarter | Stall tax from dozens of small weekly payers who almost all pay, a lottery book, loans to vendors | Wholesale brokerage, gambling dens, protecting the whole market from theft | The wholesale market |
| Palermo expansion neighborhood | Tax on building sites, selling site jobs, site guarding, theft of materials | Forced subcontracts, concrete and earthmoving, rezoning through a councillor, apartments through nominees | The construction pipeline and the planning office |
| Agricultural hinterland town | Guarding estates, water fees, livestock brokerage at the fair | Water monopoly, agricultural markets, land agency, farm subsidies, transport to city markets | The water consortium and the market |
| Coastal fishing town | Tax on boats and fuel, a share of landings, a card game on the quay, crew work on a smuggling run | The fleet as smuggling capacity, fuel depot, cannery, fish market, the landing chain from the islands | The harbor and the fleet |
| Provincial capital | Tax on cafes and shops, brokering public jobs, a betting book | Procurement, clinics, prefecture contracts, politics | The provincial administration |
| Small island | Season tax on the few businesses, boat services, landing fees, the tuna run | Almost nothing local: the money is in being the landing, storage and transfer for someone's route | The landing itself, and a partner on the mainland |

**Harbors.** A harbor is a bundle of slots: the dock labor cooperative, a customs officer, the fish market, warehouses and the fuel depot, the landing point, and later the boatyard and the ferry line. Holding it means a tax on everything that moves, jobs that become votes, cargo to steal, and capacity for smuggling. It draws its own state: customs police and coast guard, with patrols, seizures and radar as the bands rise.

**Islands and routes.** A smuggling route has two ends: a landing on an island or an open beach, and a mainland harbor. Supplier, mother ship offshore, landing, fishing boats, harbor, warehouse, distribution: every link is a slot owned by someone, and every link is where the state can hit. A mainland harbor family needs island families, through favors, kinship, a share, or a war over a rock in the sea. An island start is a smuggler's start: thin local money, one route, one partner who can replace you. Fishing seasons and the tuna run give the coast a calendar; a storm week that keeps boats in port is an event for everyone waiting on a load.

## Rank 1: Avvicinato (associate)

**Turn length:** one week, and a turn here takes one to three minutes because there is little to decide. **Expected duration:** 30 to 50 turns, roughly the first hour and a half.

**What you have.** A sponsor you are on record with, three or four small businesses on his block (a stall, a bar, a workshop) whose weekly protection tax you now collect (the larger shops pay at the three feasts, and that money is the crew chief's business), a card game you run two nights a week, a small amount of cash. No protection: if someone hurts you, your sponsor decides whether it matters.

**What a week looks like.** Planning is small. You choose which shops to visit yourself and which to send the one kid who runs errands for you. A shopkeeper is late; you decide whether to let it go a week, lean on him, or tell your sponsor. The card game needs a banker; you can bank it yourself with your cash, or ask the sponsor for a stake at a price. Then the Working Week plays: the street animates, you watch collections happen, a carabinieri patrol pass, the game fill or not. Sunday: what came in, half of it to the sponsor, the rest yours. The newspaper is one page and mostly about other people.

**What the rank teaches, one thing at a time.**
- Week 2 or 3: a late payer. The first lesson is that the protection tax is a relationship. Leaning on him works but the shop next door watches.
- Around week 6: a collector from the neighboring family's crew takes money on your block. You cannot do anything. Your sponsor takes it to his crew chief. You are not in the room; you wait outside and hear the result. The neighbor's man apologizes and returns the money; or your sponsor comes back with less territory and a grudge. Either way you have seen a dispute.
- Around week 10: the sponsor asks a favor with no money in it. Drive someone. Deliver a note. Watch a door. You do it or you do not. This is your first loyalty decision from the other side.
- Somewhere in the first quarter: your first intimidation. A shop refuses. You can break a window, or have the kid do it. The window costs nothing. The town notices anyway, a little.
- The heat lesson: a patrol stops you. Nothing happens. A week later a raid on the card game, because two weeks of visible activity in one place add up. You learn to move the game.

**Systems visible:** cash, one relationship (the sponsor), one street's worth of businesses, a whisper of local heat and of Sentiment. Nothing else is on screen.

**How you leave the rank.** Steady envelopes, no arrests, low Attention on the block, and the family accepting new men this year. Your sponsor proposes you. If the family's rule requires an act of violence for membership, that request arrives here, and it is the first choice in the game that cannot be undone.

**The initiation.** A scene: a country house, the heads of the crews, the head of family. The rules are stated aloud. You know them now as a player because you have already broken half of them by instinct as an associate and seen what it cost.

## Rank 2: Uomo d'onore (man of honor)

**Turn length:** one week, two to four minutes a turn. **Expected duration:** 40 to 60 turns, so crew chief arrives around the two to three hour mark, as the brief's onboarding target requires.

**What changes.** You now belong to a crew under a crew chief (capodecina). You have protection: nobody may strike you without the head's permission. You can have associates of your own on record. The crew chief lends you capital for a loan book at one point a week; you lend at three to five. You start the rank in debt to the man above you, which is how everyone started.

**What a week looks like.** Planning grows: your two or three associates need assigning (collections, the game, chasing a borrower). Your loan book has its own screen: who owes what, who is late, whose collateral is a van you might want. Your envelope to the crew chief is now a fixed weekly amount plus a share; you set your associates' shares and see, on the same screen, how their loyalty will move and how your envelope will look on Sunday. The Working Week now shows several streets and your men on them. Sunday shows the envelope you pay and the envelopes you receive.

**What the rank teaches.**
- Your first dispute as a party. One of your associates put a shop on record that another man in your crew says was his. You are called to the crew chief. You have three ways in: concede, pay, hold. Rank, prior claim and how much the crew chief likes each of you decide it. You lose a shop or gain one, and the other man remembers.
- Your first arrest. An associate is picked up for a beating. The arrest screen shows his estimated exposure and loyalty, the lawyer's fee, whether his mother is being looked after, and the rough odds he talks. You pay, or you do not. He comes back in a month, or he does not. If he talks, the crimes he knows about move onto your dossier. This is the Flip Roll, seen once, small.
- The code applied to you. An associate of another crew insults you in the bar. As an associate you would have hit him. Now hitting a man on record with someone else is a dispute, and killing anyone needs the head. The game makes you ask permission and shows you what asking costs.
- Peers. Another man of honor in your crew wants the same shops and the same promotion. He is polite. Your first sign that danger comes from beside as well as above.
- The household. A marriage proposal arrives, or a cousin needs a job, or your mother asks why you never come to Mass. These are small events now; they are alliance and obligation systems being introduced.
- Reputation. Rumors about you appear: "pays on time," "hot-headed," "a man of his word." You start to see what people read when they look at you.

**Systems visible:** the crew chart (your crew only), the loan book, the arrest pipeline in its simplest form, the dispute scene, reputation facets, your own exposure estimate for the first time.

**How you leave the rank.** Weight above the crew-chief threshold (men on record, envelope size, a few claims), the head's favor, and a crew vacancy. Vacancies come from arrests, deaths, shelving, promotions. The generator guarantees that your crew chief carries high exposure, so a vacancy is plausible in your first years; whether it comes to you depends on your envelopes and on who else wants it. You may also make the vacancy happen, and the game will let you, and remember.

## Rank 3: Capodecina (crew chief)

**Turn length:** a fortnight, contracting to weeks in a crisis; from here a turn takes the brief's five to ten minutes. **Expected duration:** 80 to 120 turns, the longest stretch of the climb.

**What changes.** The head hands you a crew: five to ten men of honor, their associates, a stretch of the family's territory, a meeting place. The family's expected share is set just above what the crew currently earns. Closing that gap is the whole rank. Everything you did as a man of honor is now done by your men, with your problems, and you recognize every one of them.

**What a turn looks like.** Planning is now a management screen. The territory view shows every business on your stretch with its status: paying, late, squared, refusing, owned. The crew chart shows each man with loyalty, exposure, traits and what he is doing. You set shares. You answer requests: a man wants capital for his book, another wants permission to lean on a refuser, a third wants his nephew brought in as an associate. Above you, the head's demands arrive through the underboss: a bigger envelope this quarter, a man for a job, your presence at a funeral. Design assumption, not yet decided: the number of requests surfaced per turn is capped, and the rest your men handle by their traits and you read about on the report. If so, autonomy is the game telling you which decisions matter.

The Working Week is now a fortnight and shows your whole stretch. You watch your men collect, a site get squared, a bar's back room fill, a patrol linger. On the report you get envelopes from each man, the accountant's page if you hired one, the lawyer's page if anyone is in custody, and the newspaper, now with your neighborhood in it.

**What the rank teaches.**
- Money at scale. Squeezing the stretch (higher tax, tighter shares) raises the envelope this month and lowers loyalty and compliance next month. Widening (more businesses squared, a second game, more loans) needs capital and men. The first partnership: a merchant who cannot pay becomes your silent partner; his books launder your cash, his jobs employ your men, his shop stops paying tax and the man who had it on record raises a dispute.
- The accountant and the lawyer. You hire them or you find, years later, that the family has a nephew at university. The accountant finds one of your men skimming. Now you climb the suspicion ladder for the first time: watch him, shelve him, question him, judge him. Each step costs loyalty across the crew. You might be wrong.
- The treasury. Part of your envelope goes to the common fund. When one of your men is arrested, the fund pays his lawyer and his family, if the head is running it well. If not, your man's flip risk rises and you pay from your own pocket or you watch.
- The turf. Petty thieves work your stretch. Punishing them costs men and raises compliance and Sentiment. Ignoring them makes the tax feel like theft. Civilians start bringing you disputes to settle and stolen goods to recover; these earn little money and much consent.
- Public works. The family has a seat at the contract table. You do not, but the subcontracts flow down: your stretch's earthmoving firm, your cousin's concrete. This is where the money gets real, and where your name first appears on a document.
- Attention as a band. For the first time a meter is shown, as a band with signs: quiet, noticed, watched, targeted. Your neighborhood's heat feeds the family's Attention. A public beating is one thing; a refusing merchant burned out is a headline, and headlines are the state's memory.
- The first killing, if it comes. A man of yours has been talking to a detective; or a refuser has become a symbol; or the head orders it. The operation screen: target, crew (shooters, driver, lookout, the friend who brings him), method, timing. Odds, evidence, who carries the exposure. If design treats a planned killing as a crisis (the brief lists wars, campaigns, trials and successions; a hit is the natural addition), the fortnight contracts to a week and you watch it happen or fail. Whatever happens, the newspaper reports what the street saw, the victim's kin begin a grudge, and every man who was there now knows something about you.
- The feast. The saint's committee is yours or a rival's. Sponsoring it buys Sentiment and votes and shows the neighborhood who provides.
- Danger from above. The head is bleeding the crews to pay for something. The underboss reassigns one of your men. A peer crew chief is suddenly generous with your associates. You learn to read the family chart the way you read the street.

**Systems visible:** territory management, the full crew chart, ownership, treasury contributions, accountant and lawyer, suspicion ladder, Attention band, the killing operation, the feast committee, first event chains (a refusing shopkeeper, a funeral, a protest, a squad assigned to the town).

**How you leave the rank.** Weight above the administration threshold: men on record who are yours, a stretch that earns, a partnership or two, standing with the other crew chiefs. The head's favor. And a seat in the administration opening: the underboss arrested, the counselor dead of old age. Or a coalition among the crew chiefs that wants a new administration, and a head who cannot refuse it.

## Rank 4: Sottocapo or consigliere (administration)

**Turn length:** a month, contracting to weeks in a crisis. **Expected duration:** 60 to 100 turns.

**What changes.** You see the whole family: every crew, every crew chief, the treasury, the family's territory, the district's other families. You are no longer the man who gives orders on the street; you are the man orders pass through. The Buffer is the head's dial, not yours: he can speak through you, slowly and safely, or directly, quickly and on the record. You are the buffer, and you feel both settings from inside.

**What a turn looks like.** Planning is a family chart and a district map. Crew chiefs report envelopes; you arbitrate their disputes before they reach the head. You propose policy: intake open or closed this year, the tax rate on the stretch that is refusing, whether the family enters the heroin business the neighbor is offering. You manage the treasury's obligations: the jailed men's families, the lawyers, the widows. You meet the other families' administrations at funerals, weddings and the feast. You position for succession, because everyone in the room is.

The monthly turn contracts to a week when something happens: a war between two other families in the district that the Commission asks yours to mediate, a trial of one of your crew chiefs, an election campaign the family is funding, a magistrate assigned to the province. The report is now a family report: income by crew, treasury, arrests, the newspaper's province page, the lawyer's summary of open cases, and the "what the state may know" panel with its signs.

**What the rank teaches.**
- The buffer from inside. Every order you pass on distorts a little according to who carries it and gains a little skim on the way. Every order the head gives directly is faster and lands on his dossier instead of yours. You feel this from both sides: as the intermediary who now carries the evidence, and as the man who would like the head to be careful. When you become head, this dial becomes yours.
- Arbitration. Two crew chiefs claim the same site. You decide before it reaches the head. Your decision is remembered by both and read by the others as a reputation facet: fair, or bought.
- Policy and the Commission's shadow. If the family is loud, the Commission may close intake for the district and ban what your head wanted to do. If your head wants to break a ban, you are the one who knows.
- The grey zone. You have money and time now. A nephew goes to law school; it will take years and he may become a priest instead. A cousin runs for the council with the neighborhood's votes; if he wins, rezoning follows; if he is caught, the family is in the paper.
- Kinship. Your daughter's marriage to a neighboring family's son is a treaty. A killed relative on either side outlives any treaty.
- The magistrate. If Attention has climbed, a name appears in the newspaper: an investigating magistrate assigned to the province. From now on bribes buy local blindness only. The state has a face. It can be transferred through a politician at a cost, or killed, which history shows buys years at most and brings the state down on the whole province.
- Succession. The head is aging, or in custody, or losing the crews' respect. You can wait, groom yourself as the acclaimed heir, build a coalition among the crew chiefs, court the district head, or move first. A coup can be bloodless if the Commission tolerates it and the crews accept it. If it fails, you are judged.
- Danger from above, at its sharpest. A head who suspects you can hand you to the police without a body. The signs are there: a detective who knows too much, a meeting you were not told about.

**Systems visible:** the buffer dial, the family treasury in full, policy proposals, arbitration scenes, the career system, kinship alliances, the magistrate as a character, the association case's first signs, the succession scene, the coup scheme.

**How you leave the rank.** The head dies, retires into the shelved state, is jailed under a regime that cuts his command, or is deposed. Then the family's men elect a head, usually by acclaiming the one everyone already expected. If that is you, the district head's backing and the Commission's tolerance make it stick. If it is not, you are underboss to a new man, or you are a problem he has to solve.

## Rank 5: Capofamiglia (head of family)

**Turn length:** a month, contracting to weeks in a crisis. **Expected duration:** open-ended.

**What changes.** The territory is yours. Every killing and every induction needs your approval. The district opens: the other heads of your district, the election of the district head among you, and through him the Provincial Commission. As a plain head you vote in your district and lobby the district head for everything that goes to the Commission: bans, intake, arbitration, regencies, permission to kill anyone of the state. If your Weight makes you district head, you sit at the Commission's table yourself and those become your votes. The family's votes are yours to deliver. The family's share at the contract table, and the subcontracts that come with it, are yours to negotiate. The buffer is now the central dial of your survival: how many people stand between you and what the family does.

**What a turn looks like.** Planning is a district and, as district head, a province. The district screen shows the other heads, their standing with you, their families' health and Attention, open disputes and what is going up to the Commission; the Commission screen is read-only until you are district head, then it shows the pending votes as yours. The politics screen shows the politicians who owe you and the candidates you fund, the votes the territory can deliver, and what came back: rezonings, contracts, a transferred magistrate, a fixed appeal. The family chart is now delegated: you set policy and the underboss runs it. The treasury is yours. The Attention band is the thing you look at first every month.

The report is the province's newspaper, the Commission's minutes, the treasury, the lawyer's cases, the accountant's plausibility page (your declared wealth against your visible life), and the state panel, which by now has real content if you have been loud: a dedicated squad, a magistrate's name, seizures, a collaborator.

**What the rank teaches.**
- Approval. Every request to kill comes to you. A crew chief wants a refuser dead. A neighbor wants your permission to hit a man of yours who wronged him. Your district head asks how you stand on whether a politician who failed may be killed, before he carries the district's view to the Commission. Each yes is Weight and Attention; each no is respect lost or kept.
- The district and the Commission as boards. In the district: elect the district head, settle disputes between your families, agree who bids for a weakened neighbor. Through the district head, or as district head yourself: bans, intake, who may be killed, whose territory goes to whom, regency over a weakened neighbor and later its absorption, a redrawn district, splitting your own family to make a client. Win a vote by favors owed; lose one by a reputation for greed.
- Votes. An election every few years. Deliver the neighborhood's votes to the candidate who owes you, or fund your own, at the cost of votes and money. A winner returns contracts and protection; a loser is a debt; a winner who forgets you is a liability, and removing him is the loudest thing you can do short of a magistrate.
- Heroin, if you choose it. A refinery needs a chemist, a supplier abroad, a site and a buyer in America. The money is enormous. The exposure is everywhere in the chain, and the Commission may have banned it, in which case you are running a scheme against your peers as well as the state.
- Living on the run. When a warrant is issued for you, which the top Attention bands produce, you can go into hiding inside your own territory: notes by courier, a network of suppliers, command slower and safer. Each courier is a capture risk. You may rule this way for years.
- The negotiation. At the extreme, an event chain: the state offers, or appears to offer, an easing if the violence stops. It may be real. It may be a trap. Accepting it changes how the Commission sees you; refusing it changes how the state does.
- Rise and fall around you. Families in your district weaken, fall under regency, are exterminated, dissolve. Each fall is a vacancy and a lesson, and the district decides who benefits. A collaborator in a neighbor's family produces a mass trial that reaches into yours.
- The heir. A son who is a lawyer. A nephew who is a crew chief. A protégé the crews respect. Grooming one is years of choices, and it is the only way the game continues past your own death or life sentence.
- Stepping back. When you have enough clean wealth and a low enough band, you can be shelved: legitimate business, long quiet turns, still bound, still callable, a risk that fades. The legacy screen is available at any point.

**Endings.** Killed, with or without a groomed heir. Convicted for life, with the same question. A collaborator yourself, if your family turned on you first. Shelved, watching the newspaper from a distance. Or still in the seat, thirty years on, in a family that never made the front page.

## Two ways to play the same island

**Loud.** As an associate you break the window yourself. As a man of honor you kill the man who insulted you, with permission, and the crew respects you. As a crew chief you burn out refusers and your stretch pays in full, and a squad is assigned to your town by the second year. As underboss you push the family into heroin and the money doubles. As head you are district head within five years, and a magistrate's name is in the paper, and one of your crew chiefs has been arrested with a life sentence in front of him and a mother nobody visited. The game ends in a bunker courtroom with your name first on the indictment. Weight peaked early; Attention never came down.

**Quiet.** As an associate you let the late payer slide and tell your sponsor. As a man of honor you concede the shop and gain a friend. As a crew chief you square businesses rather than burn them and put your money into a construction firm and a supermarket. As underboss you keep intake closed, pay every jailed man's family on time, and send a nephew to law school. As head you sit at the contract table, deliver votes to a councillor who becomes a regional deputy, never approve a killing the Commission did not ask for, and move by notes. The state's panel stays nearly empty for twenty years. You are shelved at sixty with a lawyer son running the firm, and the legacy screen records that no one from your family ever testified. Weight came slowly; Attention never rose.

Most runs are neither. They are a quiet man who had to be loud once, and spent the rest of the game paying for it.
