# Event Storming: The Associate Week and the Soldier's First Ten Weeks (2026-09-25)

Status: a solo event-storming session over the phase 6b/6c content (`docs/design/09-first-ranks.md`, `docs/design/10-situations.md`), read against the promise in `docs/gameplay-walkthrough.md` and the acceptance criteria in `docs/first-ranks-requirements.md`. No code changed. Evidence is cited by file and line where it matters; where a claim rests on inference rather than a direct read, it is marked as such.

Method: three passes over the same timeline, in character.

- **The player** (a strategy gamer) read `docs/first-ranks-requirements.md` §1's five rules and asked, at every moment: what do I decide, what does it cost in words I can act on, and does the *next* report tell me what my choice did?
- **The domain expert** (a Sicilian family, 1970s) read `docs/gameplay-walkthrough.md` and asked: does this feel like an envelope, a favor, a sponsor's responsibility, a slow tide of police attention — or like a spreadsheet with Italian labels?
- **The engineer** read `packages/sim/src/facts.ts`, `step.ts`, `log.ts`, `scene.ts`, `view.ts` and the template sources in `packages/content/src/templates/*.ts`, and asked of every consequence: who owns the write, what fact carries it, and by which read model does the player ever see it?

The templates themselves turned out to carry their own event-storming notes — the authors of `associate-week.ts` and `associate-people.ts` documented, inline, six gaps each where the design called for something the engine cannot yet express, and what they did instead. Those comments are primary evidence and are quoted directly below; they are the single richest source in the repo for this exercise.

Coverage evidence: `pnpm harness coverage --seeds 10 --turns 80 --strategy yesMan` and `--strategy careful`, both `RESULT: PASS`. Headline numbers used below: yesMan reaches 49 of 150 player-facing outcomes in 800 seed-turns; careful reaches 23 of 150. All 30 decision templates fired at least once across the two runs (none is silently dead), but coverage says nothing about the 12 scheduled-only follow-up templates that have no `decision` block (see Gap C).

## 2. Gaps

### (a) Events nobody can see
*Cross-checked against `packages/sim/src/log.ts` (`projectReport`), `packages/sim/src/scene.ts` (`clipOf`), and — to avoid false positives — against the other read models in `view.ts` (proposta, book, sponsor mood, state signs, `PlayerView.crises`).*

- **Found by the engineer.** `BandChange` gets a report line but **no scene clip at all** — `clipOf`'s `switch` has no `case "BandChange"`. The walkthrough's own headline dramatic beat ("a squad is assigned to your town by the second year") is text-only; nothing animates on the map when Attention crosses a band. (`packages/sim/src/scene.ts` lines ~195–241; `packages/sim/src/log.ts` line 68.)
- **Found by the engineer.** `CrisisFlagSet` has **no report line and no clip** for its own fact. Its *onset* happens to be narrated because the outcome that raises it (`dispute.claim`'s `heldLost`) writes its own `ReportNote`, and its live status is visible every turn via `PlayerView.crises` — but its **end is completely silent**: `reducers/calendar.ts` (`advanceCalendar`, lines 58–61) just decrements `ttl` and filters the flag out with no fact emitted at all. A war that started with a headline ends with nothing; the player only notices because `crises` quietly stops listing it next turn.
- **Found by the engineer.** `MoneyDestroy` — unlike `MoneyMint` and `MoneyMove` — has **no case in `projectReport`**. Every sink (a fine, a bribe, the feast box, a lawyer's fee) is visible only because the *template's own* outcome text happens to say so; a future template author who forgets the report text on a `MoneyDestroy`-only outcome would ship a silent cost (the schema's `hasChannelText` check, `schema.ts` line ~384, is the only backstop, and it is a build-time content-validation rule, not a runtime guarantee against a hand-written line that lies about the amount).
- **Found by the engineer, narrower claim.** `ClaimSet` and `ClaimRelease` are visible whenever a *template* emits them (each of the ones in §1 has its own report text), but are **silent when the player's own `claimBusiness`/`releaseClaim` action** produces them directly — see (b) below; listed here too because the fact kind itself has zero generic handling in either `log.ts` or `scene.ts` (only `ClaimTransfer` toward `me` is handled).

### (b) Commands with no visible feedback next turn
*Checked against `PlayerAction` in `step.ts` and `projectReport`/`clipOf`.*

- **Found by the player.** Three of the six `PlayerAction` kinds get **no report line at all**, ever: `setShare`, `claimBusiness`, `releaseClaim`. The requirements' rule 2 ("you see what your choice did on the next report") is written for the *card* decisions and is enforced there by content validation, but plain actions are outside that validator's reach — a player who sets a subordinate's share, claims a stall, or releases a claim has to go check the Book or Town view to confirm it happened, rather than being told.
- **Found by the player.** Asking the sponsor for anything is never a player-initiated command at all — every favor (`drive`/`note`/`door`) is something the sponsor asks the player, on a schedule the player does not control. There is no "go find work" or "ask for a job" action, so a slow week (no card spawns) cannot be turned into an active one; the player can only wait. (See also the player's missing-commands list below.)

### (c) Policies that never fire in coverage
- The coverage tool (`packages/harness/src/cli.ts`, the `coverage` case) only instruments templates that have a `decision` block — it loops `for (const t of templates) if (t.decision) {...}`. **All 12 scheduled-only, no-decision templates are invisible to it**: `assoc.sponsor.repay`, `assoc.debtor.week2`, `assoc.witness.talks`, `assoc.sponsor.returns`, `assoc.shop.reported`'s sibling `operation.arson`/`operation.bomb` (crew-chief-level, technically out of this task's rank scope but sharing the same engine), `civil.refusal.spread`, `state.squad.watch`, `assoc.sponsor.takenOn`, `assoc.run.ends`, `soldier.askAssociate.claim`, `soldier.loan.extended`, `soldier.associate.leaves`. This is a genuine blind spot: the two coverage runs cannot say whether, say, `assoc.debtor.week2`'s `ranAway` branch (which schedules `assoc.debtor.ran`) is exercised at all, only that its parent decision fired.
- Among the 30 decision templates, `family.intimidation.choose` and `dispute.claim` show **100% of their options "never chosen by the player"** in *both* runs. This is not a coverage hole in the tool — it is because both decisions route to role `chief`/`arbiter`, an NPC, not the player, at these ranks (§2(e) below explains why that itself is a hotspot).
- `assoc.sponsor.warning` and `assoc.sponsor.dropped` never appear at all in the `careful` coverage output (they are not merely under-covered — they are absent, meaning the harness never spawned them for that preset in 800 seed-turns). Cause, confirmed by reading `ai-player.ts`: `careful` is hard-coded to always accept the "quiet favor" (`assoc.favor.note`), which resets `streakRefused` to 0 on every acceptance, so the refusal counter that gates the warning (`>=3`) and the drop (`>=5`) never accumulates. Working as tuned, but it means the *only* AI preset that plays "the careful man" never actually tests the "dropped" path the requirements' story 5 asks for.

### (d) Events with no cause, or a cause that names nothing
No fact was found with a missing or empty `Cause.rule`; every system and template inspected (`systems/record.ts`, `systems/loans.ts`, `systems/succession.ts`, and the template files) supplies at least a `rule` string, and most also carry `actorId`/`instanceId`. One soft finding, not a defect: `RecordDelta` (the fact that drives la proposta) carries `cause: { rule: "record.weekly", actorId: c.id }` — a real, named cause — but produces **no reader-facing text of its own**; a paid week is only ever narrated in aggregate, through la proposta's three "signs" sentences, never as its own line ("You paid your envelope on time"). This is a read-model gap, not a broken-cause gap, and is folded into (a)'s spirit rather than double-counted.

A related, corrected-in-place finding worth recording precisely, because it shows the same gap fixed once and not reused: `associate-people.ts`'s file header (lines 43–50) documents, as of its own writing, that `RefValueSchema` had no `null` variant and that `pnpm test` failed schema validation on a literal `null` effect field — so `assoc.sponsor.dropped` was written to clear `onRecordWith` only indirectly, via `ClaimRelease`'s reducer side effect, rather than by emitting `SuperiorSet ... superiorId: null` directly. Reading `schema.ts` itself (lines 72–78) shows this was since fixed: `RefValueSchema` now includes `z.null()`, with the comment "e.g. `SuperiorSet.superiorId` null (design 09 §4, the dropped scene)" — and the newer `situations-c.ts` (design 10, `soldier.associate.leaves`, line 169) does exactly that: `{ fact: "SuperiorSet", characterId: "$man", superiorId: null, ... }`. So the capability exists and is proven working by a sibling template, but the associate-rank `dropped` scene was never migrated to use it, leaving one rank's identical domain moment ("this man is no longer under me") expressed two different ways for no remaining engine reason. Low severity, cheap fix, listed here rather than promoted to a top-5 hotspot precisely because the engine already does the right thing.

### (e) Domain moments the walkthrough promises that no event represents (the domain expert's list)
- **"You can break a window, or have the kid do it"** (`gameplay-walkthrough.md` line 49, the associate's "first intimidation"). The template that actually handles a refusing shop, `family.intimidation.choose`, decides on role **`chief`**, not the player (`docs/catalogue.md` line 97). As an associate — and, per the walkthrough, even as a soldier watching his own stall refuse — the player never personally makes this call; he only learns the result as a report line his crew chief decided. `assoc.latePayer`'s `lean` option is the nearest thing the player actually controls, and it is a much softer beat (a fear/heat bump, not an arson/bomb choice).
- **"You have three ways in: concede, pay, hold"** (walkthrough line 69, the soldier's "first dispute as a party"). `dispute.claim` decides on role **`arbiter`** (`docs/catalogue.md` line 184), not on either disputing party. A soldier who is the `holder` or `poacher` in a claim dispute is a bystander to his own dispute in the current content — the walkthrough's promised agency belongs, mechanically, to whoever is bound as `arbiter` (presumably the crew chief), not to the man the dispute is about.
- **Reputation as words** ("pays on time," "hot-headed," "a man of his word," walkthrough line 74; first-ranks §4 "shopkeepers who refuse or report acquire names and traits when they matter"). What shipped is la proposta's three signs (about the player himself) and the sponsor-mood sentence (about the sponsor, flavored by his one personality trait) — real, and reasonably close — but there is no generalized reputation facet attached to the *player* that other characters' `notes[]` arrays can quote back, the way `NOTE_TAGS` in `view.ts` (arrested/poached/conceded/paidOff) does for other people. The player is described to himself; he is never shown how the block or the crew is said to describe him.
- **"The kid... can be arrested, flip, or one day be your first associate"** (first-ranks §10, open question 2). The kid can be arrested (`assoc.kid.caught`) and can, in the `named` branch, produce testimony evidence — but nothing in the shipped content ever promotes the kid to associate; `soldier.askAssociate` always mints a brand-new `CharacterCreate` ("Turi Lo Verde"), never re-uses the kid the player has been running errands with for thirty weeks. The domain expert would call this a missed beat: the natural, personal payoff of "the boy who's been loyal to you" becoming "your own man" never fires.

### (f) Things the player would expect to be able to do and cannot (the player's list)
- **No way to ask for work.** Every favor, and the card game itself, is offered on the sponsor's schedule; there is no "go find a job" or "ask the sponsor for something to do" action for a quiet week. First-ranks §1 rule 1 promises "every week asks something of you," and the coverage data backs that up in aggregate (owner's tuning note in design 09 §12: "two or more decisions in about two thirds of weeks"), but there is still no player-initiated lever for the other third.
- **No way to check a shopkeeper's trait before deciding.** First-ranks §1 rule 3 promises "you can read the risk before you choose... this shopkeeper has reported men before." The `reporter`/`proud`/`latePayer` traits exist in the generator (design 09 §5), but — per the engineer's finding in §3 hotspot 1 — there is no relation from a business to its owning character, so no hint text or precondition can ever actually surface a specific shop's trait to the player. The decision card's hint is the same generic sentence for every shop, every time.
- **No way to see the bones rule before it matters**, contrary to the requirements' own recommendation (open question 4: "the sponsor tells you in week one, in character"). No template in `associate-people.ts` or `associate-week.ts` delivers that line; `bonesRequired` is a silent boolean the player only discovers when `assoc.proposal`'s `bones` branch forces a killing favor on him at the door of the ceremony.
- **No way to move the kid off collection duty permanently**, or to give him up — `nightOff` is a one-week choice, not a standing policy; the player re-decides the kid's week every single turn for the life of the rank, with no "set and forget" option, which for a "weekly card" (design 10 §1) is a minor but real friction against rule 1's "two or three decisions... never idle, but also never repetitive-feeling."

## 3. Hotspots, ranked

**1. A missing `Business → owner Character` relation silently drops three separate promised mechanics, and every template author who has hit it has had to invent their own workaround.** Evidence, in the implementers' own words: `assoc.latePayer`'s `lean` option drops the "if shop has trait reporter" bonus outright ("there is no relation from a business to its (nullable, lazily-created) `ownerId` character in roles.ts," `associate-week.ts` lines 156–160); `sendKid`'s botched branch redirects the shopkeeper's own memory of being laughed at onto the *player* instead, "the same 'no relation to a business's owner character' gap" (lines 188–192); and `assoc.civil.help` abandons its designed lazy `CharacterCreate` of a named shopkeeper entirely, banking the favor with the sponsor instead (`associate-people.ts` lines 267–268, 299–302). Three independent authors, three different workarounds, one root cause — and it directly blocks first-ranks §4's "the consequences are people" and the player's own missing-commands list (§2(f)). **Proposal, fitting the existing engine:** add `Business.ownerId: CharacterId | null` as a field owned by `towns` (the existing owner of `Business`), populated lazily the first time a template needs it (mirroring how the kid and rival are already lazily generated at world-gen, design 09 §5) — no new fact kind, no new engine surface, just a field and a role-selector relation (`ownerOf`) that the three existing dropped branches can then bind against.

**2. [Owner's note, same day: already fixed. `applyEffects` builds one context per effect list since the 2026-09-24 integration; the template comments the session read were stale. Kept for the record; the remaining work is to let `assoc.civil.help` and `state.patrol.stop` use it.] The `$mint:<prefix>` reference does not survive across effects in the same outcome, so `CharacterCreate` cannot safely be followed by anything that references the character it just made — an engine bug, not a missing feature, confirmed empirically by the implementer.** `associate-people.ts` (lines 27–41) documents having verified with a throwaway scheduler run that `engine/scheduler.ts`'s `applyEffects` builds a fresh `EffectContext` per effect, so a `MemoryAdd` in the same list referencing `$mint:chr`'s id gets a *different* freshly-minted id and is silently rejected as "unknown character." This is exactly the mechanism design 09 needed for a lazily-created shopkeeper or patrol officer, and its absence is the direct cause of hotspot 1's `assoc.civil.help` and `state.patrol.stop` workarounds. **Proposal:** fix `applyEffects` to build one `EffectContext` per outcome/option's effect list rather than per effect (a bug fix inside the existing engine function, not a new capability) — this alone reopens the door to hotspot 1's fix without needing the `ownerId` field for the *patrol officer* case specifically, since that one only ever needed a throwaway, non-recurring character.

**3. The exact design problem that started this whole phase — "all you do is click one button... you see what your choice did" — still has three literal blind spots: the plain player actions.** `setShare`, `claimBusiness`, and `releaseClaim` (the three non-decision entries in `PlayerAction`, `step.ts` lines 30–36) produce `ShareRuleSet`, `ClaimSet`, and `ClaimRelease` facts that `projectReport` never narrates (§2(b)) — unlike every situation card, which the content validator (`schema.ts`'s `hasChannelText`/rule 2 check) guarantees a report line for. A player who sets a subordinate's cut, claims a stall, or gives one up gets no confirmation on the next turn's report at all; he has to go check the Book or the Town view to see whether it took. This directly contradicts first-ranks §1 rule 2, the very rule the phase 6 playtest verdict was written to fix, and it is the one place in this whole session where the fix that was made (situation cards) provably did not reach every kind of player command. **Proposal, fitting the existing engine:** extend `projectReport`'s `if (f.kind === ...)` chain (`log.ts`, alongside the existing `MoneyMint`/`MoneyMove`/`LoanOpen` cases) with three more generic cases — `ShareRuleSet` where `superiorId === me`, `ClaimSet` where `holderId === me`, `ClaimRelease` where the released claim's prior holder was `me` — each producing one plain sentence. No new fact kind, no new template, a read-model addition of exactly the shape already used four times over in the same function.

**4. The rank's own stated finale — the ceremony — and the game's central slow-building dread — the Attention band rising — both land as text with no animated beat, while smaller, low-stakes moments (a card game payout, a kid's errand) do get one.** `RankChange` and `BandChange` have no case in `scene.ts`'s `clipOf`, and `CrisisFlagSet`'s end is silent in every read model (§2(a)). Design 11 §1 promises "nothing drawn contradicts the report" as an acceptance bar, which is technically met (nothing *wrong* is drawn), but the inverse — nothing *notable* is drawn for the two moments the walkthrough spends the most words on — undercuts design 07's whole premise that the map is where consequences are felt. **Proposal:** add `clipOf` cases for `RankChange` (kind `"claim"` is already the closest existing bucket for "something changed hands"; a new clip kind `"ceremony"` fits the existing `ClipKind` union pattern) and for `BandChange` (a `"sign"`-visibility clip reusing the existing `HeatDelta`-below-threshold pattern, since Attention rising is exactly the kind of thing the design already treats as a sign, not a labelled fact) — both are read-model additions inside the existing `SceneClip` shape, no new engine type.

**5. Two decisions the walkthrough frames as the player's own — the associate's first intimidation and the soldier's first dispute as a named party — are, in the shipped content, decided by an NPC role instead, and coverage confirms it: both templates show 100% of their options "never chosen by the player" across both AI presets.** `family.intimidation.choose` decides on role `chief`; `dispute.claim` decides on role `arbiter` (both `docs/catalogue.md`, cited in §2(e)). This is the sharpest player/domain-expert agreement in the whole session: both roles expected personal agency at exactly these moments, and the engine currently gives it to someone else. **Proposal:** change `decision.role` selection so that when the player-controlled character *is* the business's collector (for `family.intimidation.choose`) or *is* one of the two named parties (for `dispute.claim`'s `holder`/`poacher`), the decision routes to `me` instead of `chief`/`arbiter`, falling back to the existing NPC routing only when the player is a bystander — this is a role-selector change on two existing templates, not a new template or a new engine feature, and it would need its own scenario test per the content rule (design 06 §5) plus a `careers` check that the player-routed branch is reachable by a normal collector/associate before crew-chief rank.
