# The Sicilian Cosa Nostra, 1950s to 2020s: Design Research Report

Research date: 2026-09-21. Sicily-only deep dive backing the Sicily setting decision in `docs/brainstorm-requirements.md`.


# THE SICILIAN COSA NOSTRA, 1950s-2020s: A DESIGN RESEARCH REPORT

## 1. Structure and ranks

**The family (famiglia, cosca, borgata).** The basic unit is territorial, not kin-based: one family per town, or per Palermo borgata/quartiere. Its members are *uomini d'onore* (also "soldati"). Members are grouped in *decine* (nominally ten, in practice 5-30) each led by a *capodecina* appointed by the boss. The family is run by the *capofamiglia* (also "rappresentante"), who is formally *elected* by the men of honour of the family; he appoints a *sottocapo* (vicecapo) and is flanked by one or more *consiglieri*, who are elected by the soldiers rather than appointed (it.wikipedia "Famiglia (mafia)"; Treccani "Nello Stato di Cosa Nostra"). Buscetta and later pentiti describe the election as real but usually pre-negotiated: in a stable family the outgoing boss's designated successor is acclaimed; in a contested one the vote is where factions form. The DIA's recent figure of 78-81 families and roughly 1,540 affiliates for Palermo province gives an average family of about 20 men, though historic Palermo families such as Santa Maria di Gesù under Bontate or Ciaculli under Greco ran to 100-200 members. Small hinterland towns have families of 5-15. Buscetta estimated the whole island at 3,000-5,000 initiated members; the standard modern estimate is ~5,500 in about 100 families (en.wikipedia "Sicilian Mafia").

**Mandamento and capomandamento.** Three or more territorially contiguous families form a *mandamento*. Its head, the *capomandamento*, is chosen by the capifamiglia of the constituent families (it is normally the strongest of them) and sits on the Provincial Commission. He serves until death, arrest-induced replacement, or removal by the Commission (it.wikipedia "Mandamento (cosa nostra)"). The mandamento system was set up in the late 1950s on the American model after the October 1957 Grand Hotel des Palmes meeting with Joseph Bonanno; the Palermo Commission ("Cupola") formed 1957-58, was dissolved after the 1963 Ciaculli bomb and the ensuing crackdown, re-formed in 1969-70 as a triumvirate (Bontate, Badalamenti, Riina) and then as a full Commission from 1974-75. The *Commissione interprovinciale* or *Regione*, coordinating provincial commissions, was created in 1975 at Riina's suggestion; Buscetta and Calderone describe it as dominated by Palermo. The Commission's core functions: approve murders of men of honour from other families, approve any killing of state officials, magistrates or politicians ("omicidi eccellenti"), settle territorial disputes, and ratify new capimandamento.

**Families and mandamenti per province (DIA-era mapping, roughly stable since the 1980s):**
- Palermo: 15 mandamenti (8 in the city, 7 in the province), 78-81 families. City mandamenti: San Lorenzo-Tommaso Natale, Resuttana, Noce-Cruillas, Passo di Rigano-Boccadifalco, Porta Nuova, Pagliarelli, Villagrazia-Santa Maria di Gesù, Ciaculli-Brancaccio. Province: Corleone, San Giuseppe Jato and Partinico (recently merged as Camporeale), Bagheria, Misilmeri-Belmonte Mezzagno, Trabia, San Mauro Castelverde (Addiopizzo "15 mandamenti gestiti da 81 famiglie"; PalermoToday DIA 2024).
- Trapani: 4 mandamenti, 17 families. Trapani (Trapani, Custonaci, Paceco, Valderice); Alcamo (Alcamo, Calatafimi, Castellammare del Golfo); Mazara del Vallo (Mazara, Marsala, Salemi, Vita); Castelvetrano (Castelvetrano, Campobello di Mazara, Gibellina, Partanna, Salaparuta, Poggioreale, Santa Ninfa). Trapani was historically the most "Palermo-orthodox" province and Messina Denaro's power base (TrapaniOggi DIA).
- Agrigento: 7 mandamenti (older maps list 10) and about 42 families, coexisting with the Stidda (GrandangoloAgrigento).
- Caltanissetta: 4 mandamenti (Gela, Vallelunga, Riesi, Mussomeli), 15 families; Gela is the Stidda's stronghold.
- Catania: one dominant Cosa Nostra family (Santapaola-Ercolano) plus the Mazzei ("carcagnusi", a second Cosa Nostra-affiliated family), surrounded by non-Cosa Nostra clans (Cappello, Laudani, Cursoti, Pillera). No mandamento structure.
- Messina: families only, no mandamenti; the Barcellona Pozzo di Gotto family ("barcellonesi"), the Mistretta and Tortorici groups (later the "mafia dei pascoli"), with influence from both Palermo and Catania.
- Enna: families only (Enna, Barrafranca, Pietraperzia, Villarosa), historically subordinate to Caltanissetta.
- Siracusa and Ragusa: weak or absent Cosa Nostra; Catania clans and the Gela Stidda (Vittoria) project influence (Wikipedia "Mandamento", La Spia "mappa"; DIA relazioni).

**The Stidda as rival.** Born in the early 1980s from men expelled ("posati") by Cosa Nostra or orphaned by the Corleonesi's purge of Giuseppe Di Cristina's Riesi network, the Stidda ("star"; members tattoo a five-point star between thumb and index finger) has no Commission, only allied autonomous clans, and cared less about "honour" than about profit. The Gela feud with Cosa Nostra (December 1987 to early 1991) left about 120 dead in Gela alone; the broader Stidda-Cosa Nostra war in Caltanissetta, Agrigento and Ragusa (Vittoria, Niscemi, Palma di Montechiaro) exceeded 300 deaths. The Stidda killed judge Rosario Livatino on 21 September 1990. Estimated size ~2,000 (en.wikipedia "Stidda"; IrpiMedia; lavialibera).

**Territory.** Boundaries follow administrative or historic lines: a comune, or a Palermo borgata whose name is often that of an old rural hamlet absorbed by the city (Ciaculli, Brancaccio, Resuttana, Passo di Rigano). Everything economic on the turf, legal or illegal, is the family's business: every shop, building site, market stall and public contract owes the family; any outside criminal (thief, drug dealer, even another family's man) must ask permission and pay. Gambetta's framing is useful for a game: the mafia sells *protection*, and its core asset is a monopoly of protection in a bounded territory.

## 2. Rules and rituals

**Punciuta.** The initiation described identically by Buscetta (1984), Calderone, Mannoia, Brusca and the Lo Piccolo "decalogue" of 2007: the candidate, sponsored by a man of honour who vouches for him after a probationary period as *avvicinato* (associate), is brought before the boss and at least three members. His trigger finger is pricked (with a thorn, sometimes from the bitter-orange tree, or a pin), blood is dripped on a holy image (a *santina*, often the Madonna of the Annunciation, Cosa Nostra's patron, feast 25 March), the image is burned in his cupped hands while he swears: "Le mie carni debbono bruciare come questa santina se non manterrò fede al giuramento." Brusca's 1976 initiation used the same formula (Stampacritica "Le regole"; en.wikipedia "Sicilian Mafia").

**Stated rules (Buscetta 1984; Lo Piccolo decalogue 2007):** one never presents oneself to another man of honour; a third person who knows both must introduce them with "chistu è a stissa cosa" ("this one is the same thing"). Absolute silence toward outsiders, absolute truth among members: liars ("tragediaturi", those who set up "tragedie" or false accusations) are killed. Never look at the wives of friends; treat wives with respect; no affairs. Never be seen with police; never go to the state's justice (the traditional exception was reporting a stolen car). Appointments must be kept; one must be available to Cosa Nostra even if one's wife is giving birth. No admission of anyone with a relative in the police, anyone with a "traitor" in the family, anyone of bad conduct. Men of honour may not kill other men of honour without the capofamiglia's consent, and may not kill outside their territory without the local boss's consent. The historic ban on killing women, children, magistrates and journalists was a norm not a law; the Corleonesi tore it up: Riina ordered relatives of Buscetta and Contorno killed in 1982-84 (Buscetta lost two sons, a brother, a nephew, a son-in-law and others), Mannoia's mother, sister and aunt were shot in 1989 after he turned, and Giovanni Brusca's men held 12-year-old Giuseppe Di Matteo for 779 days before strangling him and dissolving him in acid on 11 January 1996 to punish his pentito father Santino (Antimafia Duemila; en.wikipedia "Santino Di Matteo").

**Posato.** A member "shelved" is stripped of standing without being killed: he may no longer be introduced, attend meetings, or earn; he remains bound by silence. Posare happens for cowardice, indiscipline, taking a mistress conspicuously, or when a faction loses. The Stidda was largely built from posati (en.wikipedia "Stidda").

**Who approves a killing.** Inside the family: the capofamiglia. A man of honour of another family: his own boss must consent, otherwise the Commission. State officials, politicians, journalists, "eccellenti" of any kind: the Provincial Commission, formally unanimous. In practice under Riina (1981-93) the Commission ratified what the Corleonesi had already done; Buscetta's testimony that the Commission approved Dalla Chiesa and the other excellent cadavers was the basis for the "teorema Buscetta" that convicted the whole Commission at the Maxi Trial (Wikipedia "Maxiprocesso").

**Messa a posto.** The formula spoken to a merchant or contractor: he must "mettersi a posto" (square himself) or "help the friends in prison" (the *contributo per i carcerati*, cash to prisoners' families). Once "a posto" the business is protected: no thefts, disputes settled, competitors kept out, and the family's word (*la parola*) binds every member of the organisation not to touch it. The "word" also settles debts and business disputes between civilians who ask the family to arbitrate: this is the honour economy that made Cosa Nostra socially useful (lavialibera "In Sicilia il pizzo non muore mai"; Today.it "come funziona il sistema").

## 3. Economics

**Pizzo: levels.** The 2008 Fondazione Chinnici / University of Palermo study (La Spina), based on 130 court files and a 1,602-firm sample 1990-2007, found Palermo pizzo worth about **175 million euros a year**, average **827 euros a month**, with tariffs by type: street vendors 60/month; retail shops 457; wholesalers 508; hotels and restaurants 578; construction firms 2,534/month, i.e. **2-4 percent of contract value**, up to 17,000/month on motorway works (Vita.it). SOS Impresa (2009) gave Palermo ranges: ordinary shop 200-500, central-street shop 750-1,000, supermarket ~5,000, building site ~10,000 per month; Sicily 50,000 victim businesses (70 percent of Italy's 150,000), and about 80 percent of Palermo shopkeepers paying in the 2000s (Famiglia Cristiana; en.wikipedia "Addiopizzo"). In 2026 Palermo cases still show demands of 5,000 euros a month "negotiable" plus one-off "adjustments" of 30,000-50,000 (Giornale di Sicilia). Payments are seasonal: Christmas, Easter, Ferragosto, when the "contributo" is collected. Collection is delegated to a low-level *esattore* (often a non-member) who visits each business; the *capodecina* aggregates; the boss keeps a *libro mastro* of who pays what (Lo Piccolo's ledgers seized 2007 listed hundreds of payers). Refusal escalates: glue in shop locks, a burnt car, a bottle of petrol or a bomb at the shutter, then shooting; Libero Grassi was killed in 1991 after refusing the Madonia family. Pizzo also "marks territory" against rivals: whoever collects in a street owns it.

**Public procurement and the tavolino.** Angelo Siino, a Baucina contractor and rally driver, was accredited by Riina in the mid-1980s as Cosa Nostra's "minister of public works." His system, in force roughly 1986-1991 for works above about 5 billion lire: contractors met "at the table" (tavolino) to pre-agree the winner of each tender by rigged rotation, involving major northern firms in the "cartel of the privileged." Split of the contract value: **3 percent to the local mafia family, 2 percent to politicians, a further 2 percent to Riina and the Corleonesi**, plus about 0.5-1 percent to Siino as coordinator; the winner then subcontracted earthmoving, concrete, guardiania (site security) and transport to mafia-owned firms and hired locals designated by the family (WikiMafia "Angelo Siino"; Editoriale Domani). After Siino's 1991 arrest, control passed to Provenzano's men (the "Pino Lipari"/Aiello network), who shifted to a subtler model: fewer bosses at the table, professionals (engineers, accountants) as intermediaries, and infiltration of the ATI consortia. The 1991 ROS "mafia-appalti" report on this system is central to the Borsellino-murder literature (Referio "dossier mafia-appalti").

**Construction and the Sack of Palermo.** Between 1959 and 1963, with Salvo Lima as mayor and Vito Ciancimino as public-works assessor, Palermo issued 4,205 building licences, 80 percent to five pensioners fronting for others (3,011 in a single month). Liberty villas along Via Libertà and the Conca d'Oro citrus belt were bulldozed; the builder Francesco Vassallo (ex-cart driver) and the VA.LI.GIO axis (Vassallo-Lima-Gioia) profited; the La Barbera, Greco and later Bontate families controlled sand, cement and labour (en.wikipedia "Sack of Palermo"). Contemporary echo: the earthmoving and concrete monopoly persists in every DIA report.

**Cigarette smuggling (1950s-70s).** Until 1960 loads came from the free port of Tangier; afterwards from Yugoslavia, Albania and Greece. Mother ships anchored in international waters and fast "scafi blu" ran cases ashore near Naples, Palermo and the small islands. Palermo's Kalsa and Acquasanta families (Tommaso Spadaro, Nunzio La Mattina, Pippo Calò, Giuseppe Savoca) partnered with Michele Zaza's Camorra from the early 1960s; in the peak early 1970s a single mother ship discharged around 40,000 cases per voyage, and Naples had ~5,000 smugglers. Revenue funded the first heroin purchases (storienapoli.it; Il Mattino "CrimiNapoli"; it.wikipedia "Pizza connection").

**Heroin (late 1970s-1980s).** Sicilian traffickers bought morphine base from Turkish supplier Yasar Musullulu in Switzerland; Marseille chemists (later Sicilians like Francesco Marino Mannoia) refined it in laboratories at Trabia and Piraineto near Punta Raisi (both found 1980, run by Gerlando Alberti), Alcamo (1985), Bagheria, and around Palermo; by official estimate Sicilians controlled ~30 percent of US heroin. Mannoia refined 600 kilos of morphine base in under two years and was paid 5 million lire per kilo; Bontate reportedly kept 10 billion lire in cash; Falcone estimated the Inzerillo-Spatola-Gambino network moved **US$600 million of heroin a year** to New York via pizzerias (the Pizza Connection, indicted 1985). Proceeds banked in Lugano and Zurich (Italia Mistero "Mannoia"; en.wikipedia "Stefano Bontade", "Salvatore Inzerillo"). The heroin windfall is what destabilised the Commission: the Corleonesi, poorer and rural, resented the Palermo aristocracy's monopoly of the American channel.

**Later drug wholesale.** After the 1980s Cosa Nostra lost heroin primacy to the 'Ndrangheta and became a wholesale buyer of cocaine and hashish; DIA 2024 notes Palermo mandamenti want "to return as world leaders in trafficking" via US Inzerillo contacts (PalermoToday DIA 2024).

**Loansharking, gambling, other.** Usury flows from pizzo: a merchant who cannot pay is lent money at 10-20 percent a month and eventually surrenders his business. Illegal lotteries, clandestine betting, later slot machines and online betting: Benedetto Bacchi, the "re delle scommesse" (700 betting shops), was convicted in 2021 (18 years, reduced on appeal) for financing the Resuttana and Santa Maria di Gesù families; confiscation 2.8-6 million (PalermoToday; LiveSicilia).

**Agriculture, water, markets.** The 19th-century *gabelloti* (estate lessees who ran latifundia for absentee barons with armed *campieri*) are the mafia's origin; in the 1950s the mafia took over water sales in the Conca d'Oro and the Palermo wholesale fruit market, Ciaculli's tangerine trade (Greco family), and the cattle and meat trade. Land reform 1950 and the "Cassa per il Mezzogiorno" turned rural mafias toward public money.

**Subsidy fraud.** EU agricultural funds via AGEA: the Nebrodi "mafia dei pascoli" (Tortorici clans) grabbed pastureland by forged leases and threats to claim CAP payments; the "Nebrodi" trial gave 91 convictions and 600+ years (2022), "Nebrodi 2" 45 convictions and 325 years (September 2026) (AGI; L'Indipendente; IrpiMedia). Regional vocational-training funds ("formazione professionale") were another Siino-era racket.

**Later rackets.** Waste hauling and landfills; wind farms: Vito Nicastri of Alcamo, "re dell'eolico", had 1.3 billion euros seized in 2010-13 as Messina Denaro's investor (Today.it; Sole 24 Ore); supermarkets: Giuseppe Grigoli's Despar chain (Trapani-Agrigento-Palermo), 12 companies, 220 buildings and 133 plots worth 700 million confiscated 2013, the Belicittà mall in Castelvetrano alone yielding 250,000 euros a month (Il Fatto Quotidiano); healthcare: Michele Aiello's Villa Santa Teresa oncology clinic at Bagheria, Provenzano's front, 15.5-year sentence in the "Talpe alla DDA" trial together with regional president Totò Cuffaro (it.wikipedia "Michele Aiello").

**Wealth and confiscation.** Messina Denaro's network is estimated at ~4 billion euros (Il Sole 24 Ore). Riina's census listed 300 flats, 38 plots and 1,685 hectares, ~80 million; the visible confiscations since 2019 total a few million (PalermoToday). Bontate's group made "hundreds of billions of lire" from heroin; Inzerillo funnelled it into construction. Italy-wide ANBSC holds tens of thousands of properties; Sicily alone has 6,437-7,727 real-estate units already assigned and 8,206-8,656 awaiting, plus 451 firms assigned and 884 under management (Libera/Vita.it, 2025). SOS Impresa's total "Mafia SpA" racket-plus-usury turnover was estimated at 23 billion nationally.

## 4. Politics

**The exchange.** Votes for contracts, licences, jobs, and trial "adjustments." A Palermo family could deliver a few hundred to a few thousand preference votes (DIA and Siino: whole-island Cosa Nostra packages in the 150,000-200,000 range at peak); families instructed members' relatives and dependents, distributed pre-marked ballots and sample "santini elettorali", and escorted voters. Lima's DC current, allied with Andreotti nationally from 1968, held Palermo's town hall (Lima mayor 1958-63, 1965-68; Ciancimino mayor 1970) and the regional assembly. Ciancimino was convicted of mafia association in 1992; Giovanni Gioia was the third leg (WikiMafia "Voto di scambio"; en.wikipedia "Salvatore Lima").

**Aggiustamento.** Corrado Carnevale, president of the Cassation's first criminal section, annulled hundreds of mafia convictions 1987-92 on formal grounds ("ammazzasentenze"); pentiti named him as Cosa Nostra's guarantor in Rome via Lima and Andreotti. He was convicted at second instance (6 years) and acquitted definitively in Cassation. In January 1992 the Maxi Trial was assigned to a different section and the life sentences were confirmed on 30 January 1992; Lima was shot on 12 March 1992 in Mondello for failing to deliver, and the Cassation's 2001 Lima judgment adopts this motive (Il Post; ArchivioAntimafia). Andreotti's 2004 Cassation outcome: crimes before spring 1980 time-barred, later charges acquitted, with the court finding "organic relations" with Cosa Nostra until 1980 (it.wikipedia "Processo Andreotti"). Marcello Dell'Utri, Berlusconi's Palermo-born associate and Forza Italia co-founder, was definitively sentenced in 2014 to 7 years for concorso esterno as mediator between Cosa Nostra and Berlusconi's companies 1974-92 (it.wikipedia "Marcello Dell'Utri").

**Comitato d'affari.** The model that emerged from Siino and the appalti inquiries: a standing cartel of politicians, entrepreneurs, professionals and mafia bosses who jointly allocate public money; the mafia is one shareholder, often not the largest. Freemasonry served as the neutral room: Siino himself was a mason; the 2017-18 Antimafia Commission (Bindi) seized lodge rolls in Sicily and Calabria and found 193 members in mafia proceedings, concluding that the organisations were sometimes indistinguishable (Camera dei Deputati 2017; Editoriale Domani).

## 5. Violence

**Rates.** Palermo city recorded 70 murders by 8 October 1981 (one every four days) and 100 by 26 August 1982; contemporaries and the Wikipedia consensus put the Second Mafia War (April 1981 to 1983/84) at "over 1,000" dead island-wide, with a sceptical low estimate of 400-1,000, almost all Bontate-Inzerillo men, since the Corleonesi struck without retaliation. After 1984 mafia killings fell, then rose again nationally: 577 mafia murders in Italy in 1990, 718 in 1991 (Progetto San Francesco; it.wikipedia "Seconda guerra di mafia"; "Collaboratore di giustizia").

**Lupara bianca.** Disappearance without a body: strangling and dissolving in acid, or burial in the countryside. In the 30 November 1982 "pulitina di piedi" (foot-cleaning) about a hundred of Rosario Riccobono's Partanna-Mondello men were killed and only a dozen bodies were found. The Corleonesi used it to deny the state a corpse and the victim's family a funeral (WikiMafia "Seconda Guerra di Mafia").

**Cadaveri eccellenti and consequences:**
- Cesare Terranova (judge, 25 Sept 1979) and Boris Giuliano (police chief, 21 July 1979): the first killings of the heroin era; no legislative response.
- Piersanti Mattarella (regional president, DC reformer, 6 Jan 1980) and Gaetano Costa (chief prosecutor, 6 Aug 1980).
- Pio La Torre (PCI regional secretary, 30 Apr 1982) and prefect Carlo Alberto Dalla Chiesa (3 Sept 1982): the Rognoni-La Torre law, stalled since 1980, passed ten days after Dalla Chiesa, 13 September 1982.
- Rocco Chinnici (chief investigating magistrate, car bomb 29 July 1983): Caponnetto arrives and formalises the antimafia pool.
- Giuseppe Montana (28 July 1985) and Ninni Cassarà (6 Aug 1985), police: Falcone and Borsellino moved to Asinara to write the Maxi indictment.
- Salvo Lima (12 Mar 1992): the state's guarantor eliminated.
- Giovanni Falcone (Capaci, 23 May 1992) and Paolo Borsellino (via D'Amelio, 19 July 1992): the Scotti-Martelli decree of 8 June 1992 (41-bis hard prison, converted 7 Aug 1992), the army in Sicily from 25 July 1992, the DIA's activation, and mass pentitismo.
- Don Pino Puglisi (15 Sept 1993) after the Pope's Agrigento "convertitevi" of 9 May 1993; Cosa Nostra bombed churches in Rome that July.

**Strategy.** The Corleonesi (Riina) practised terror: eliminate rivals, then attack the state to force it to negotiate (the 1993 mainland bombs in Rome, Florence and Milan, 10 dead). After Riina's arrest on 15 January 1993, Provenzano imposed *sommersione* (submersion): no more excellent murders, low-profile extortion at moderate rates so victims do not report, reinvestment in legal business, dialogue over violence, "pax mafiosa" (swissinfo; Editoriale Domani). Homicides in Palermo fell to single digits per year in the 2010s.

## 6. The state as opponent

**Forces.** Polizia di Stato (Squadra Mobile Palermo, the Cassarà-Montana generation), Carabinieri (ROS from 1990; Dalla Chiesa's legacy), Guardia di Finanza (asset tracing), all pooled from 29 October 1991 in the **DIA**, an interforce body under the Interior Ministry (decree 345/1991). Prosecution was centralised in the **Direzione Nazionale Antimafia** (decree 367/1991) with District Antimafia Directorates (DDA) at each appeal-court seat (Camera dossier GI0133; DIA site).

**Antimafia pool.** Chinnici's idea (1980-83): several investigating magistrates share every mafia file so no one man is indispensable. Caponnetto (1983-88) ran it with Falcone, Borsellino, Di Lello, Guarnotta. Falcone's method: follow the money (bank records, the Spatola case) and cross-check pentiti.

**Rognoni-La Torre (Law 646, 13 Sept 1982).** Art. 416-bis: membership in a mafia-type association (defined by intimidation, subjection and omertà) is itself a crime, with higher penalties for leaders and for armed associations; plus patrimonial measures: seizure and confiscation of assets disproportionate to declared income. **Law 109/1996** (from a Libera petition of one million signatures) mandates social reuse of confiscated property; ANBSC was created in 2010 to manage it (lavialibera; Camera "Beni confiscati").

**Maxi Trial.** Indicted 475 (460 tried), 200+ lawyers, a purpose-built bunker courtroom at the Ucciardone; 10 Feb 1986 to 16 Dec 1987: 344 convictions, 19 life sentences, 2,665 years; upheld by Cassation 30 January 1992 (it.wikipedia "Maxiprocesso"). Its mechanics: a single indictment (8,000 pages) proving the organisation's existence and Commission responsibility for every ordered killing.

**Pentiti law (DL 8/1991, Law 82/1991).** Sentence reductions (up to one-third, life to 12-20 years), protection programme run by the Servizio Centrale di Protezione, relocation, new identities (Law 45/2001 tightened it: 180-day deadline for full statements). Counts: 1,277 collaborators and 4,915 relatives under protection in 2016; Cosa Nostra 258, Camorra 504, 'Ndrangheta 176 (it.wikipedia "Collaboratore di giustizia"). Motivations: Buscetta (1984) after the Corleonesi killed 11 relatives and after Falcone's respectful approach; Contorno the same; Mannoia (1989) fearing death in the Corleonesi purge; Cancemi (1993) walked into a Carabinieri barracks fearing Riina would kill him; Brusca (1996) after arrest, to save his brother and lessen his sentence; Giuffrè (2002) after arrest; Spatuzza (2008) via religious conversion. The pattern for a game: cooperation spikes when a member fears his own side more than prison.

**41-bis.** Suspension of ordinary prison rules for mafia convicts: isolation, one monitored visit a month, censored mail, no contact with other bosses; made permanent in 2002. It cut the boss's command channel and triggered the 1993 bombings as Cosa Nostra's attempt to get it lifted (it.wikipedia "41-bis").

**Vespri Siciliani (25 July 1992 to 8 July 1998).** About 6,200 soldiers on the ground daily, 150,000 rotated in total, guarding courts, homes of magistrates and sensitive sites, freeing police for investigation (it.wikipedia "Operazione Vespri siciliani").

**Wiretaps and pizzini.** Since bosses stopped using phones, prosecutions rely on ambient bugs in cars and homes and on seized paper: Provenzano's typed *pizzini*, numbered and coded with Bible verses, carried by a chain of *postini* through farms around Corleone; his 43-year run ended on 11 April 2006 at Montagna dei Cavalli, 2 km from his birthplace, through following the laundry sent by his wife (en.wikipedia "Pizzino"; PalermoToday).

**What worked / what did not.** Worked: 416-bis plus asset confiscation (attacks the motive), pentiti plus the pool method (attacks the secret), 41-bis (attacks command), civil-society refusal plus consumer pressure (attacks the revenue base), DIA-style interforce coordination. Did not: mass arrests without economic follow-up (1963-69 crackdown, acquittals at the 1968 Catanzaro trial), Cassation formalism, the army as a symbolic presence, and the 1990s "trattativa" ambiguity that Cosa Nostra read as weakness.

## 7. Civil society

**Church.** Long ambivalent (Cardinal Ruffini in 1964 called the mafia a Northern invention), the Sicilian Church turned under Pappalardo (his 1982 "Sagunto" homily at Dalla Chiesa's funeral), then John Paul II at Agrigento (9 May 1993) and Puglisi's martyrdom in Brancaccio (15 Sept 1993; beatified 2013). The 2014 Francis excommunication statement and the 2017 ban on mafiosi as godfathers in some dioceses follow. Church festivals remain contested ground: processions "bowing" before bosses' homes (Avvenire; en.wikipedia "Pino Puglisi").

**Libero Grassi.** Palermo textile manufacturer; published "Caro estortore" in the Giornale di Sicilia on 10 January 1991 refusing to pay the Madonia family; Confindustria's local leaders sneered; he was shot on 29 August 1991. His isolation is the founding trauma of the anti-racket movement (Fondazione Falcone; gds.it).

**Addiopizzo.** Founded 2004 by young Palermitans who papered the centre with stickers: "Un intero popolo che paga il pizzo è un popolo senza dignità." The "Pago chi non paga" consumer campaign launched May 2006 with 100 pizzo-free businesses; by the 2020s more than 1,000 businesses and about 13,000 consumers had signed, with Addiopizzo staff accompanying victims to file complaints and to court. The 2008 baseline was ~80 percent of Palermo businesses paying; investigators and Addiopizzo now describe compliance as roughly 50-60 percent in Palermo, near-total in markets like Ballarò ("A Ballarò pagano tutti", GdS Sept 2026) and much lower in the "pizzo-free" corridors of the centre. Reports rose from a handful per year in the 1990s to dozens per year (Addiopizzo; LiveSicilia).

**Confindustria Sicilia.** Under Ivan Lo Bello (2006-) it adopted in 2007 a code of ethics expelling members who pay pizzo or fail to report it, and had 18 Caltanissetta firms expelled within a year; his ally Antonello Montante's later arrest (2018) for a spying network damaged the movement's credibility (it.wikipedia "Confindustria Sicilia"; La Sicilia).

**Palermo Spring.** Leoluca Orlando's mayoralty (1985-90, again 1993-2000, 2012-22): breaking with Lima's DC, cleaning municipal contracts, the "Comitato dei Lenzuoli" sheets hung from balconies after 1992, school programmes and the Falcone Tree.

**Women.** Felicia Bartolotta Impastato, mother of Peppino (killed by Badalamenti in Cinisi, 9 May 1978), turned her house into a memorial; Rita Atria, 17, daughter and sister of Partanna mafiosi, testified to Borsellino and killed herself a week after his murder (26 July 1992); Piera Aiello, her sister-in-law, became the first testimone di giustizia in Parliament (2018). Antimafia in schools is institutional since the 1980 regional law and Libera's 1995 founding ("Libera. Associazioni, nomi e numeri contro le mafie"), with 21 March as the day of memory.

## 8. Geography and daily texture

**Palermo city.** The San Lorenzo-Tommaso Natale mandamento (Lo Piccolo, Mondello coast, Partanna); Resuttana (Madonia); Noce-Cruillas; Passo di Rigano-Boccadifalco (Inzerillo, the American link); Porta Nuova (Calò, the Ucciardone and the historic centre with the Vucciria and Ballarò markets); Pagliarelli (Motisi); Villagrazia-Santa Maria di Gesù (Bontate's "prince" domain by the cemetery); Ciaculli-Brancaccio (Greco's Favarella estate, later the Graviano brothers). Each borgata has its piazza, its church and its market street where the esattore is seen.

**Hinterland.** Corleone (Riina, Provenzano, Liggio); San Giuseppe Jato (Brusca; Falcone's killers, the Capaci detonator); Partinico (Vitale); Bagheria (Provenzano's base in the 1980s, Michele Greco's Villa Valguarnera area, the "camera della morte" of Filippo Marchese at Sant'Erasmo was in Palermo); Villabate (the 1990s Provenzano hub for the mall project); Misilmeri and Belmonte Mezzagno (Benedetto Spera); Altofonte (the Di Matteos).

**Trapani.** Castelvetrano and Campobello di Mazara (Messina Denaro, arrested 16 Jan 2023 after 30 years living under the name Bonafede a few kilometres from home); Mazara del Vallo (fishing fleet, Agate); Alcamo (Nicastri's wind farms; the 1985 refinery). **Agrigento**: Porto Empedocle, Canicattì, Palma di Montechiaro, Ribera; the Stidda in Palma and Canicattì. **Caltanissetta**: Gela's petrochemical plant and its subcontracts as pizzo base; Riesi, Vallelunga (Madonia). **Catania**: Santapaola-Ercolano, the "quattro cavalieri del lavoro" (Costanzo, Rendo, Graci, Finocchiaro) who kept Santapaola on the payroll for their hotels and public works; Pippo Fava murdered 5 Jan 1984 for writing it (it.wikipedia "Quattro cavalieri dell'apocalisse mafiosa"). **Messina**: Barcellona, the Nebrodi pastures.

**Coasts and islands.** The Egadi, Aeolian islands, Pantelleria and Lampedusa served as transhipment points for cigarettes and later hashish from Tunisia and Libya; Mazara and Trapani fishing boats as carriers. Smuggling coves: Balestrate and Terrasini (west), Sant'Erasmo and Acquasanta (Palermo), Capo d'Orlando, the Gela plain.

**Meeting places.** Country houses and *fondi* (Michele Greco's Favarella estate at Ciaculli hosted the Commission and a heroin lab), hunting lodges (Santapaola-Graci hunts), citrus warehouses, stables, the back rooms of bars and the Ucciardone prison (in the 1970s bosses dined on lobster inside). Bosses spent *latitanza* not abroad but in their own territory: Riina 23 years in Palermo villas, his children born and baptised at the hospital; Provenzano 43 years among Corleone farms; Messina Denaro 30 years in Campobello. This requires *vivandieri* and *favoreggiatori* who carry food, medicine, women, pizzini and rent houses: Bernardo Riina of Corleone had 15 million euros seized for supporting Provenzano. Funerals of bosses and feast-day processions are shows of strength and moments of truce; the Madonna dell'Annunziata (25 March) is Cosa Nostra's own feast (Monreale News; PalermoToday).

## Gameable mechanics for a Sicily-set game

1. Ranks ladder: avvicinato (associate) -> uomo d'onore after punciuta (needs a sponsor and a "proof") -> capodecina (appointed by boss) -> consigliere (elected by soldiers) -> sottocapo (appointed) -> capofamiglia (elected by family members, usually the pre-agreed heir) -> capomandamento (chosen by 3+ capifamiglia) -> seat on the Commission.
2. Family size 5-30 in towns, 20-60 in Palermo borgate, 100-200 for great families; a decina is nominally 10 but flexes 5-30.
3. Territory is total: every licit and illicit activity inside the boundary owes the family; outsiders operating there without "permission" create a casus belli.
4. Pizzo tariff table by business type (2000s euros/month): stall 60, shop 200-500, prime-street shop 750-1,000, restaurant/hotel ~580, supermarket ~5,000, building site 2-4 percent of contract or ~10,000; collect at Christmas, Easter and Ferragosto.
5. Compliance: ~80 percent of Palermo shops paid in 2008; model Addiopizzo-style consumer campaigns and Confindustria expulsion rules as levers that drive compliance toward ~50-60 percent, with markets like Ballarò staying near 100 percent and pizzo-free corridors dropping much lower.
6. Refusal escalation ladder: glue in locks -> arson of car -> bomb at the shutter -> murder; each step raises reporting risk and civil-society sentiment.
7. Messa a posto grants a protection buff (no theft, dispute arbitration, competitor exclusion) so some merchants seek it voluntarily; the family's "word" must be honoured or reputation collapses.
8. Public works: winners pre-agreed at the tavolino; take 3 percent for the local family, 2 percent to politicians, 2 percent to the dominant faction, ~1 percent to the coordinator, plus forced subcontracts (earthmoving, concrete, guardiania, transport) worth far more than the cut.
9. Political currency: a family delivers a block of preference votes (hundreds to low thousands); in exchange it gets licences, contracts, jobs and, at high tiers, "aggiustamento" of trials in Cassation.
10. A politician who fails to deliver on an aggiustamento can be killed only with Commission approval and triggers a state backlash (Lima 1992 -> stragi -> 41-bis and the army).
11. Killing state officials, magistrates or journalists requires Commission approval and raises a "state heat" meter that unlocks new laws: 416-bis and confiscation (1982), pool method (1983), pentiti law (1991), 41-bis and army (1992).
12. Excellent-cadaver consequences should be delayed but heavy: the Rognoni-La Torre law passed ten days after Dalla Chiesa; 41-bis six weeks after Falcone.
13. Homicide intensity is the state's trigger: a Palermo year above ~100 murders (1981-82 levels) brings a national crackdown; single-digit years (Provenzano era) let the state's attention drift.
14. Two grand strategies: Terror (Corleonesi) with fast territorial gains but exponential heat and pentiti; Submersion (Provenzano) with moderate pizzo, no excellent murders, legal reinvestment, slower growth and lower risk.
15. Lupara bianca as an option: removing a body denies evidence (lower heat) but denies a funeral (higher grievance among the victim's kin, higher pentito risk).
16. Rule violations (killing women/children, killing without approval, lying to peers) lower internal legitimacy and make members more likely to flip.
17. Pentito risk for each member = fear of own side (purges, kin killed) + arrest with heavy sentence + broken family bonds; the 1991 law makes flipping much more attractive once 41-bis and life sentences are in play.
18. Roughly 1 in 20 members ends up a collaborator (258 Cosa Nostra collaborators against ~5,500 members); a single high-rank pentito (Buscetta) can trigger a Maxi Trial that removes the whole Commission.
19. 41-bis severs the imprisoned boss's command link; leadership passes to a reggente and factions may split.
20. Confiscation targets assets disproportionate to declared income; fronting through "puliti" (clean nominees) reduces risk but adds a nominee-betrayal chance (Grigoli, Nicastri, Aiello cases).
21. Confiscated assets do not vanish: they become state/social assets (Libera cooperatives) that reduce mafia legitimacy in the borgata.
22. Latitanza in-territory: a fugitive boss keeps ruling via pizzini and postini at a cost in "vivandieri" upkeep; each courier is a capture-risk node (Provenzano fell through his laundry chain).
23. Pizzini and bugged cars: communications choice trades speed (phones, meetings) against interception risk; paper is slow but safe until seized.
24. Drug era shock (late 1970s): heroin refineries yield enormous cash (US$600 million/year for one network) concentrated in the American-linked Palermo families, destabilising the Commission and inviting a rural faction's coup.
25. Diversification menu by era: cigarettes (1950s-70s), construction and the Sack (1957-70), heroin (1977-85), public works cartel (1985-92), supermarkets/wind/health/betting/EU subsidies (1995-2020s).
26. Provincial personalities: Palermo (Commission politics), Trapani (orthodox, loyal, business-heavy), Agrigento/Caltanissetta (Stidda insurgency and feuds), Catania (business-mafia symbiosis with the cavalieri), Messina/Enna (weak, subsidy fraud), Siracusa/Ragusa (open, contested).
27. Stidda mechanic: expelled or "posato" members can join a loose rival network with no Commission; wars with it (120 dead in Gela 1987-91) drain both sides and invite police.
28. Church and festivals: a hostile bishop or a Puglisi-type priest reduces recruitment in the borgata; killing him is a catastrophic legitimacy hit.
29. Freemasonry as a neutral meeting room: lodge membership speeds deals with politicians and contractors but leaves lists that can be seized.
30. Civil-society events (Grassi's murder 1991, the 1992 sheets, Addiopizzo 2004, Confindustria 2007) each shift a city-wide "sentiment" variable that governs reporting rates and vote packages.
31. The Church, the pentiti and the DIA are exogenous escalations the player cannot buy off, only anticipate: model them as timed thresholds tied to cumulative violence.
32. Smuggling geography: Egadi, Aeolians, Pantelleria, Lampedusa and the Mazara fleet as route nodes with variable interdiction risk; mother-ship loads of ~40,000 cases per voyage in the 1970s.
33. Hits on politicians earn no money but remove a blocker; hits on merchants earn nothing and raise sentiment; the only profitable violence is intimidation that does not have to be executed.

## Open questions / suggested follow-up
- Year-by-year Palermo homicide counts for 1981-1991 were not found in an online table; the ISTAT "Delitti denunciati" series or Alfio Caruso's "Da cosa nasce cosa" would give them. - Vote-package sizes per family are anecdotal in the sources found (Siino, Calderone, Cancemi testimony); the Andreotti and Dell'Utri judgments on archivioantimafia.org contain the detailed figures if the design needs them.
